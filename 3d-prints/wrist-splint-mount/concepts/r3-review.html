print workflow (the reveal)

  1. Slice elements only, flat. In Bambu Studio right-click the layer slider at layer 2 → Add Pause.
  2. Print the 2 base layers; the printer parks.
  3. Lay tulle over the whole plate, pull taut.
  4. Pin it with small neodymium magnets to the steel plate, outside the print area, clear of the nozzle path.
  5. Resume. The next layer welds through the mesh; remaining layers sandwich the fabric.
  6. Cool, peel, trim tulle at the edge binding and inside the thumb opening and strap slots (sharp scissors or a hot knife seals polyester edges).

supplies

  • Tulle: plain polyester tulle, fine mesh, no glitter (glitter blocks the weld; too-fine organza doesn't bond). One bolt is a lifetime supply for this part (~200 × 180 mm per attempt). Amazon search: polyester tulle bolt 54"
  • Magnets: neodymium discs, N35 or stronger, ~6×3 mm (the size the prior-art model below calls for), 10–14 pcs. Amazon search: neodymium disc magnets 6×3 mm
  • Already on hand: 68D TPU-for-AMS spool, 11 mm velcro strap, the X1C's magnetic steel plate.

prior art (verified technique)

  • Printables: “Hex pattern for tulle” — same recipe: pause at height, tulle pinned with 6×3 N35 magnets to a magnetic PEI plate, keep the fabric dead flat.
  • Prusa forum: printing over tulle — magnets outside the build area; mesh lets melt flow through and bond mechanically.
  • 3DPrinterly: How to 3D print on fabric (8 steps) — the general pause-and-lay workflow, taut fabric, few base layers.

open before export

  • 20-min test square first: traces + one loaded strap tab + a diamond on scrap tulle; yank the tab before trusting a spinning motor to it.
  • Tulle bond with 68D TPU at its nozzle temp — prior art is mostly PLA; verify on the test square.
  • Flip-for-left-hand check still applies (fabric side vs element side now differ — elements face OUT, tulle sits on skin).
